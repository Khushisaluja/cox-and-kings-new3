Cox & Kings' central UX problem is not aesthetics — it is the 2020 NCLT insolvency, where travellers lost deposits. Every page aimed at a high-value buyer is really answering "will you still exist when I travel, and is my money safe?" The About page at `/about-us1` is the dedicated answer to this.

**Why:** A ₹8–15 lakh multi-generation booking is made 12–14 months ahead. The buyer is underwriting the company's survival, not just buying a holiday.

**How to apply:** When reviewing any Cox & Kings trust/About/brand page as Rajesh, check for these five things, which have been *absent* in past versions and are the highest-leverage fixes:
1. Where the deposit is held (escrow / trust account / any financial protection) — the page tells you to *ask* the question but never *answers* it.
2. Verifiable identifiers: Indian entity registered name + CIN/GSTIN, Singapore parent UEN, IATA/TAAI membership numbers shown *before* payment, not "on your booking confirmation".
3. Named, photographed humans with checkable roles (leadership + tour managers), not initials-in-a-circle.
4. Hard numbers behind soft claims — "capped in size" with no cap, "premium" with no comparison.
5. Cancellation/refund terms and visa-delay handling.

Also standing: dated commitments must be checked against the *current* date — "In 2026" copy reads as stale once the year is underway.
